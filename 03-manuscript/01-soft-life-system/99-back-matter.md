### Premium Offer Worksheet

Answer these questions to design your premium offer:

1. **Outcome:** What specific, measurable result will your client achieve?
2. **Time:** How long will it take, and what's the time commitment for the client?
3. **Mechanism:** What's your unique process or method for delivering the result?
4. **Proof:** What evidence do you have that this works? (Testimonials, case studies, your own results.)
5. **Price:** What is the value of this outcome to your client? What would they gladly pay?
6. **Bonuses:** What could you add that increases value without increasing your workload?
7. **Guarantee:** What risk can you remove for the client?

### Boundary Scripts

**Response-time boundary:**
"Thanks for reaching out! I check messages on [DAYS/TIMES] and respond within [RESPONSE TIME]. If it's urgent, please [PROCESS]. Otherwise, I'll get back to you in the order received."

**Scope boundary:**
"I'd love to help with that! It falls outside our agreed scope, so I can add it as a separate add-on for [PRICE] or include it in a future phase. Which would you prefer?"

**Availability boundary:**
"I'm available for calls on [DAYS] between [HOURS]. Outside those hours, I'm focused on client work and rest. You can book a time here: [LINK]."

**Saying no to a client:**
"Thank you for thinking of me. I'm not able to take this on right now, but I'd be glad to recommend someone or keep you in mind for [TIMEFRAME]."

**Saying no to a project:**
"That sounds like a great opportunity, but it's not the right fit for me at the moment. I hope it goes wonderfully."

## A Note on Sources

This book is a synthesis of established ideas in productivity, business design, and sustainable work, combined with the patterns I have observed working with solopreneurs. Where I reference research or expert thinking, I have chosen ideas that are widely accepted and practically useful rather than academic.

The frameworks in this book are original syntheses, but they stand on the shoulders of good thinking about attention, systems, leverage, and burnout. Readers who want to go deeper will find the following books useful.

**On focus and attention**
- *Deep Work* by Cal Newport — the case for protected, undistracted work.

**On pricing, offers, and profit**
- *Profit First* by Mike Michalowicz — a cash-management system for small businesses.
- *The Pumpkin Plan* by Mike Michalowicz — how to focus on the clients who matter most.

**On habits and sustainable performance**
- *Atomic Habits* by James Clear — how small systems shape behavior.
- *Burnout* by Emily Nagoski and Amelia Nagoski — the physiology of stress and recovery.
- *The 4-Hour Workweek* by Timothy Ferriss — the book that popularized location-independent, leveraged work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**I'm just starting out. Is this book for me?**

Yes — but with a caveat. If you haven't started yet, you have an advantage: you can build the right architecture from the beginning instead of untangling the wrong one. Start with Part II and design your one offer before you take on clients. The rest of the book will make more sense once you have something to systemize.

**I have a service business, not a product. Does this work?**

Yes. The SOFT Method is designed for service businesses — coaching, consulting, design, writing, virtual assistance, and other expertise-based work. The goal is not to turn your service into a product. It's to productize your service: clear scope, repeatable delivery, premium pricing.

**How long until I can work fifteen hours a week?**

It depends on where you're starting. Some solopreneurs get there in ninety days. Others need six months or a year. The timeline matters less than the direction. Every system you build, every task you transfer, every boundary you set moves you closer.

**What if I can't afford to delegate?**

You don't have to start with a hire. Start with the Transfer Ladder: eliminate, automate, template, then delegate. Automation and templates are free or cheap. When you're ready to delegate, start with a few hours a week — not a full-time hire. Many solopreneurs begin with a virtual assistant for five hours a week.

**What if my clients expect 24/7 availability?**

They expect it because you've trained them to. The good news is that you can retrain them. Set a response-time policy, communicate it clearly, and honor it consistently. Most clients will adjust. The ones who won't are usually not the clients you want.

**What if I have multiple offers I love?**

You can keep them — but not all at once, and not for everyone. Choose one core offer for your business. Keep the others as ascension offers, seasonal offers, or personal projects. The key is clarity: your audience should know what you're known for.

**What if I'm in a busy season?**

Then simplify even more. Busy seasons are not the time to build elaborate systems. They're the time to protect your minimum effective week, delegate what you can, and survive. The full redesign can wait for a calmer season.

**Do I need to be on social media?**

No. You need a way to reach your audience, but it doesn't have to be social media. Email, referrals, partnerships, speaking, and SEO are all valid channels. Choose the one that fits your energy and your audience — and do it consistently.

**What tools do I need?**

Start with the minimum viable stack: a scheduling tool, an email platform, a place to store files, and an invoicing tool. Add tools only when they solve a real problem. The goal is fewer, better systems — not a complicated tech stack.

**What if I get off track?**

You won't fail at building a soft life — but you will have setbacks. You might need to adjust. You might take longer than ninety days. The direction is always available to you. Every small change — one boundary, one template, one automation — is progress. And progress compounds.
