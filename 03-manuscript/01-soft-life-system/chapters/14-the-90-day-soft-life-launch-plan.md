# Chapter 14: The 90-Day Soft Life Launch Plan

Amara wanted to change everything at once.

She wanted a new offer, a new website, new systems, new boundaries, a new schedule. She wanted to wake up tomorrow and have a soft life business.

She lasted nine days. Then she collapsed under the weight of her own ambition and went back to doing everything the old way.

"I don't understand," she said. "I know what to do. Why can't I just do it?"

Because transformation doesn't happen all at once. It happens in sequence. You don't build a house by throwing all the materials in a pile and hoping they arrange themselves. You build it one stage at a time: foundation, frame, walls, roof.

The 90-Day Soft Life Launch Plan is your sequence. It takes everything in this book and turns it into a realistic, week-by-week path from overwhelmed to operating.

Ninety days. Twelve weeks. Three phases. One soft life.

---

## Phase 1: Days 1–30 — Foundation

The first thirty days are about clarity. You're not building systems yet. You're deciding what the business is.

### Week 1: The Audit
Complete the Soft Life Audit from Chapter 3. Track your time, money, energy, and joy for one week. Calculate your Soft Life Scorecard.

**Deliverable:** A completed audit and a list of your top ten Grunt tasks.

**Time investment:** 3–5 hours.

### Week 2: Simplify
Choose your one audience, one offer, and one outcome. Write your one-sentence business. Identify your core offer and your two ascension offers.

**Deliverable:** Your one-sentence business and Offer Tree.

**Time investment:** 3–5 hours.

### Week 3: Offer
Redesign your core offer using the Premium Offer Formula: Outcome + Time + Mechanism + Proof. Set your pricing for a soft life — charge more, serve fewer.

**Deliverable:** A redesigned premium offer with clear pricing.

**Time investment:** 4–6 hours.

### Week 4: Package
Create your offer one-pager. Write your sales page or proposal template. Update your website and social profiles to reflect the new offer.

**Deliverable:** A packaged offer you can sell.

**Time investment:** 5–8 hours.

By the end of Phase 1, you have a clear, premium offer that you're excited to sell.

---

## Phase 2: Days 31–60 — Systems

The second thirty days are about building the machine. You're turning your offer into a repeatable delivery process.

### Week 5: Fulfill — Map and Template
Map your delivery process from intake to follow-up. Build three templates: a welcome email, a proposal, and a follow-up email.

**Deliverable:** A mapped delivery process and three core templates.

**Time investment:** 4–6 hours.

### Week 6: Fulfill — SOPs and Checklists
Write one SOP for your most repeated task. Create a delivery checklist and an onboarding checklist.

**Deliverable:** One SOP and two checklists.

**Time investment:** 3–5 hours.

### Week 7: Transfer — First Automation
Build your first automation. Start with scheduling and a welcome email. Connect your booking link to your calendar and email.

**Deliverable:** A working scheduling and welcome automation.

**Time investment:** 3–5 hours.

### Week 8: Transfer — First Delegation
Choose one task to delegate. Document it using the SOP you wrote. Find a contractor or VA. Hand off the task with a checklist.

**Deliverable:** One delegated task with a documented process.

**Time investment:** 4–6 hours.

By the end of Phase 2, your delivery is systemized and you've started building your invisible team.

---

## Phase 3: Days 61–90 — Freedom

The final thirty days are about designing the life. You're putting the fifteen-hour week and the quiet growth engine in place.

### Week 9: The 15-Hour Week
Design your Soft Life Week. Assign your four work modes. Set your no-work zones. Write your Boundary Policy.

**Deliverable:** A calendar-based fifteen-hour week and a one-page Boundary Policy.

**Time investment:** 3–4 hours.

### Week 10: The Automation Stack
Audit your tools. Build your second automation — invoicing, follow-up, or content scheduling. Cancel any tools you're not using.

**Deliverable:** A lean automation stack with two working automations.

**Time investment:** 3–5 hours.

### Week 11: The Quiet Growth Engine
Define your three content pillars. Create your lead magnet. Set up your Simple Funnel: Content → Lead Magnet → Email → Offer.

**Deliverable:** A lead magnet and a simple funnel.

**Time investment:** 5–7 hours.

### Week 12: Review and Refine
Complete your first Soft Life Review. Measure your hours, revenue, leads, and energy. Identify what's working and what needs adjusting. Celebrate your progress.

**Deliverable:** A completed review and a plan for the next ninety days.

**Time investment:** 2–3 hours.

By the end of Phase 3, you have a business that runs on systems, a week that protects your time, and a growth engine that works while you rest.

## Weekly Milestones and Metrics

You can't improve what you don't measure. Here are the metrics to track weekly throughout the ninety days:

### 1. Hours Worked
Track your actual hours. Are you moving toward fifteen? Are you working more than you intended?

### 2. Revenue Per Offer
Track how much each offer generates. Is your premium offer selling? Are your ascension offers working?

### 3. Leads
Track how many people enter your funnel each week. Are your content and lead magnet working?

### 4. Client Satisfaction
Ask for feedback. Are your clients happy with the delivery experience?

### 5. Energy Score
Rate your energy from 1 to 10 each day. Are you building a business that energizes you or drains you?

### 6. Joy Score
Rate your joy from 1 to 10 each week. Are you actually enjoying the soft life you're building?

The metrics are not about hustling harder. They're about noticing what's working and adjusting what isn't.

## Common Pitfalls

The 90-day plan is designed to be realistic, but you'll still face obstacles. Here are the most common pitfalls — and how to avoid them.

### Pitfall 1: Trying to Do Everything at Once
You'll be tempted to skip ahead, to build all the systems in week one, to launch everything simultaneously. Don't. The sequence matters. Trust the process.

### Pitfall 2: Perfectionism
You'll want your offer to be perfect before you sell it. You'll want your systems to be flawless before you use them. Don't wait. Build, test, refine. Imperfect action beats perfect inaction.

### Pitfall 3: Skipping the Audit
The audit feels like a delay. It's not. It's the foundation. Without it, you'll build systems for the wrong problems.

### Pitfall 4: Not Communicating Boundaries
You'll set boundaries but forget to tell anyone. Boundaries only work when they're communicated. Put them in writing. Say them out loud.

### Pitfall 5: Building Before Selling
You'll be tempted to build all the systems before you have clients. Don't. Build while you sell. The best systems are built in the trenches.

### Pitfall 6: Going It Alone
You'll try to do all of this by yourself. Don't. Find an accountability partner, a mentor, or a community. Share your progress. Ask for help.

## The Commitment

Ninety days is long enough to build something real and short enough to stay focused.

But it only works if you commit.

Not perfectly. Not without setbacks. But consistently. Week by week. Step by step.

Here's what I want you to do: write down your start date. Put the twelve weeks in your calendar. Tell someone you're doing it. And then begin.

You don't have to be perfect. You just have to start.

## The Weekly Check-In Ritual

The 90-day plan works best when you check in weekly.

A weekly check-in is a short, focused conversation with yourself about how the plan is going. It takes fifteen minutes and keeps you on track.

Here's a simple structure:

**1. What did I complete this week?**
Celebrate your progress, even if it's small. Did you finish the audit? Write your one-sentence business? Build a template? Acknowledge it.

**2. What did I skip or delay?**
Be honest. What didn't get done? Why? Is it a priority, or can it wait?

**3. What's the one thing I need to do next week?**
Choose the most important task for the coming week. Just one. Don't overwhelm yourself.

**4. What's blocking me?**
Is there a decision you're avoiding? A tool you need? A conversation you're dreading? Name the blocker and make a plan to address it.

**5. How do I feel?**
Rate your energy and joy. Are you pushing too hard? Are you taking care of yourself? The plan is not worth your health.

The weekly check-in is not a performance review. It's a navigation tool. It helps you adjust course before you drift too far.

## What to Do When You Fall Behind

You will fall behind. It's not a matter of if — it's a matter of when.

Life happens. Clients get demanding. You get sick. The plan slips. And then the shame creeps in: *I'm already behind. What's the point?*

Here's what to do when you fall behind:

**1. Don't abandon the plan.**
The worst thing you can do is throw the whole plan away because you missed a week. A missed week is not a failed plan. It's a data point.

**2. Identify what caused the delay.**
Was it a real emergency? A lack of clarity? A task that was too big? Understanding the cause helps you prevent it next time.

**3. Choose the smallest possible next step.**
Don't try to catch up on everything at once. Choose one small task and do it. Momentum matters more than completion.

**4. Adjust the timeline.**
If you need more than ninety days, take more than ninety days. The plan is a guide, not a prison. The goal is transformation, not a perfect record.

**5. Forgive yourself.**
You're building a business and a life. It's messy. It's unpredictable. You're not a machine. Give yourself the same grace you'd give a friend.

The 90-day plan is not about being perfect. It's about making progress. If you fall behind, get back up. That's the whole game.
