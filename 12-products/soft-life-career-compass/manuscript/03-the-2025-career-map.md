# The 2025 career map

This is the part where the fantasy meets the spreadsheet. All pay figures below
are BLS OEWS May 2025 national medians. Growth and openings are BLS Employment
Projections 2025–35. The Soft Life Score uses the formula from Chapter 2.

Start with the group view, then the ranking, then the full pay range, then the trade-off map.

## The four tracks

The 24 roles fall into four broad tracks. The averages below are simple
unweighted means, not market averages. They are a quick way to see the shape of
each track before you read individual profiles.

{{CHART_TRACK_SUMMARY}}

**Data & Tech** has the highest average median pay ($106,320) and the highest
average Soft Life Score (69.1). It also has the widest entry range: a data
scientist and a technical writer sit in the same track but require very
different preparation.

**Corporate & Operations** is the most portable track. Median pay averages $92,665
and the average score is 67.3. It contains some of the most portable roles in
the guide: management analyst, project management specialist, HR specialist,
and executive assistant.

**Wellness & Care** is not a monolith. The average median is $79,684, but it
ranges from $45,330 for skincare specialists to $136,570 for optometrists. The
average score is 63.7. This is the track where viral salary claims are most
likely to be wrong.

**Creative & Client** has the lowest average median ($66,195) and the lowest
average score (58.3). It also has the most variable income and the highest
share of self-employment. It can be a good life, but it is a weaker first move
if you need stable cash flow quickly.

## The full ranking

{{CHART_SCORE_RANKING}}

The ranking is not a list of “best jobs.” It is a list of roles that balance
pay, schedule, energy, entry, and demand under one set of weights. A role near
the bottom can still be the right choice if you already have the skills, the
network, or a specific life constraint that the score does not capture.

Notice what is near the top. Data scientist, management analyst, optometrist,
web and digital interface designer, computer systems analyst, actuary,
statistician, training and development specialist, information security
analyst, and technical writer.

Notice what is near the bottom. Public relations specialist, skincare
specialist, travel agent, graphic designer, and massage therapist. These are not
bad jobs. They are jobs where the median pay, the demand picture, or the entry
economics make the soft-life case harder to prove.

## The full range, not just the median

{{CHART_SALARY_RANGES}}

The median is a useful anchor, but the spread is where the real decision lives.
Data scientists range from $67,240 at the 10th percentile to $199,130 at the
90th. Optometrists range from $74,870 to $202,180. Technical writers range from
$57,440 to $145,270.

That spread is not a ladder you can climb automatically. It reflects seniority,
location, employer, specialism, and contract. A role with a high median can
still start below your enough number. A role with a modest median can have a
strong top end for people who build a specialism or a client base.

The spread also exposes the weakness in a single salary claim. “Technical
writers earn $90,000–$105,000” describes a slice near the median. It does not
describe the 10th percentile or the 90th. It does not tell you which end is
available to you this year.

## The pay–schedule trade-off

{{CHART_PAY_SCHEDULE}}

The horizontal axis is median pay. The vertical axis is schedule control. The
top-right quadrant is the dream: high pay and high control. The top-left
quadrant is the “soft but not rich” zone. The bottom-right is the “golden
handcuffs” zone: good pay, little control. The bottom-left is the “rebuild”
zone.

Most roles sit in the middle. That is the honest finding. There are few
six-figure, fully flexible, low-entry jobs. The careers that look like that on
social media usually depend on seniority, a rare employer, or a business that
is already working.

## What the data changes

Three findings are worth sitting with.

**1. The wellness premium is smaller than the research suggested.** Exercise
trainers and group fitness instructors have a median of $47,160, not
$60,000–$100,000. Skincare specialists have a median of $45,330, not
$60,000–$125,000. The top of those markets exists. It is not the median.

**2. The healthcare ceiling is higher than the research suggested.**
Optometrists have a median of $136,570, above the research range of
$90,000–$130,000. Dental hygienists have a median of $98,100, inside the
research range, with a 90th percentile above $126,000.

**3. Remote work is uneven.** Computer and mathematical roles have a much
higher telework rate than healthcare or personal care. A role can be
“remote-friendly” in theory and still require local licensing, in-person
training, or a client base. The role profile tells you which constraint applies.

## The honest conclusion

There is no single career that solves the soft life. There are better and worse
bets. The better bets tend to have:

- a median above your “enough” number;
- a schedule you can protect;
- an entry path you can afford;
- demand that is not collapsing;
- and work you can recover from.

The rest of this guide helps you find the three bets worth testing.
