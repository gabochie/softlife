# Negotiate the conditions, not just the number

A higher salary cannot fix a schedule that has no room for your life. The
strongest negotiation asks for the conditions that make the work sustainable
and frames them as a business case, not a personal favour.

Use the BLS median as context, not as your anchor. Your anchor is the value you
bring, the market for your specific skills, and your enough number.

## Before the conversation

Write four numbers:

1. Your **walk-away number**: the minimum you can accept without resentment.
2. Your **target number**: the number that makes the move worth it.
3. Your **market range**: the BLS 10th–90th percentile for the matching role,
   adjusted for your city, seniority, and industry.
4. Your **conditions budget**: the schedule, location, or support you would
   trade a small amount of salary for.

Then write one sentence:

> “I am looking for [target] with [condition], because [specific value I bring].”

## Script 1: Remote or hybrid

> “I want to make sure I can do this role at the level you need. The work is
> [deliverable-heavy / collaborative / independent], and my best output happens
> when I have [two uninterrupted days / a predictable remote block]. Can we
> agree on a 90-day trial with [two remote days] and a review against
> [specific outcomes]?”

Why it works: it offers a trial and a metric instead of an identity debate.

## Script 2: Flexible hours

> “My schedule needs to flex around [care / health / commute]. I can commit to
> being available for [core hours] and delivering [specific outcome] by
> [deadline]. Would a core-hours arrangement work?”

Do not say “I need flexibility” without a boundary. Say what you will protect
and what the team gets in return.

## Script 3: Part-time or reduced load

> “I want to stay and do this work well. I am proposing a [0.8 FTE / four-day]
> schedule for the next six months, with [scope change] and [success measure].
> If the outcomes hold, can we review the arrangement?”

Part-time is easier to approve when it comes with a scoped reduction, not the
same workload at a lower salary.

## Script 4: Workload

> “I can do A and B well, or A, B, and C at a lower standard. Which matters most
> this quarter?”

This is not a refusal. It is a prioritisation request. Put it in writing after
the meeting.

## Script 5: Salary

> “Based on the scope of this role and the market range for [occupation] in
> [location], I am targeting [number]. I have [specific evidence]. Is there
> flexibility in the base?”

If the answer is no, ask about:

- a signing bonus;
- an early review at six months;
- additional paid leave;
- professional development budget;
- remote or flexible days;
- a title change that supports the next move.

## Script 6: Promotion

> “I want to be considered for [role] in the next cycle. The difference between
> my current scope and that role is [gap]. I propose [specific project] to close
> it. Can we agree on the evidence you would need to see by [date]?”

Promotion conversations fail when they are about time served. Make them about
scope and evidence.

## Script 7: The trial

> “Before we make a permanent change, can we run a 60-day trial with [specific
> arrangement] and review [specific metrics]? If it is not working, we revert
> without drama.”

The trial lowers the risk for both sides. Use it for remote work, reduced
hours, a new role, or a return after leave.

## When they say no

A no is information. Ask:

- “What would need to be true for this to be possible?”
- “Is this a no for now, or a no for this role?”
- “What trade-off would make it work?”

If the answer is still no, you have learned something about the employer. That
is useful. It may be the evidence you need to move.

## The negotiation checklist

- [ ] I know my enough number.
- [ ] I know my walk-away number.
- [ ] I have the BLS range for the matching occupation.
- [ ] I have one page of evidence.
- [ ] I am asking for a condition, not just a feeling.
- [ ] I have a trial or review date.
- [ ] I have the offer in writing.
- [ ] I have checked the benefits and the total package.
- [ ] I have a decision date and a backup plan.

> **Action:** Write your next negotiation as one sentence: “I am asking for
> [condition] because [business reason], and I will measure it by [metric].”
