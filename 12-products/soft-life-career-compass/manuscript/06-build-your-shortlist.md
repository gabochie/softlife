# Build your three-career shortlist

The goal of this chapter is not to choose a career. It is to choose three
directions worth testing. Testing is cheaper than regret.

Set a timer for 30 minutes. Answer in order. Do not edit yourself while you
write.

## Step 3: Filter the 24 roles

Go back to the profiles. Mark every role:

- **GREEN** if the median is above your enough number and the catch is
  survivable.
- **AMBER** if the work fits but the pay, entry path, or schedule needs a
  compromise.
- **RED** if the catch is a dealbreaker.

You should have no more than eight greens and ambers. If you have more, raise
your enough number or tighten your non-negotiables.

## Step 5: Design three tests

A test is a small, time-boxed action that produces evidence. It is not “do more
research.” Use one of each:

| Test | Question it answers | Timebox |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conversation | Do people doing this job describe a life I want? | 2 weeks, 3 conversations |
| Artifact | Can I produce the core output at a basic standard? | 2–4 weeks |
| Real-world test | Will someone pay for this, hire me for it, or let me shadow? | 4–6 weeks |

## Step 6: Set the decision rules

Before you start testing, decide how you will decide. Write your rules here:

- I will continue a direction if ___________________________________
- I will pause a direction if _____________________________________
- I will stop a direction if ______________________________________
- The maximum I will spend testing all three directions is ____________
- The latest date I will make a direction decision is ________________

## The three-test rule

Do not quit your job after one good conversation. Do not abandon a direction
after one awkward informational interview. Run all three tests. Then decide
with evidence.

The point is not certainty. The point is to stop guessing.
