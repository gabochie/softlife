# The 90-day career pivot

Most career changes fail for a boring reason: they are designed as a leap
instead of a sequence. This plan assumes you are still working. It asks for
about five hours a week, not a heroic life overhaul.

The output after 90 days is not a new job. It is a decision you can defend, a
small piece of proof, and a network that knows what you are moving toward.

## The weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** 30 minutes planning.
- **Tuesday and Thursday:** 90 minutes of focused work.
- **Saturday:** 2 hours for conversations, practice, or applications.
- **Sunday:** 30 minutes review.

Protect the time. If a week collapses, do the smallest version, not nothing.

## Days 1–14: Audit the current life

**Goal:** understand what is actually costing you energy.

- Track your time for five working days. Include commute, care work, and
  recovery.
- Rate each workday from 1 to 5 on energy at 6 p.m.
- List the three tasks that drain you most and the three that make time pass
  well.
- Calculate your enough number from Chapter 6.
- Write your non-negotiables.

**Output:** a one-page current-life audit.

**Do not:** use this as a reason to quit. Use it as a specification.

## Days 15–30: Validate the shortlist

**Goal:** replace assumptions with conversations.

- Book three conversations per shortlisted direction. Use the scripts in
  Chapter 8 and the questions in the back matter.
- Ask about the ordinary week, not the highlight reel: schedule, energy,
  management, entry path, and what they wish they had known.
- Ask for one introduction to someone with a different employer or contract.
- Record the answers in the same format for every role.

**Output:** nine conversation notes and one updated shortlist.

**Kill criterion:** if three people in a direction describe a life you do not
want, remove it.

## Days 31–60: Build one proof artifact

**Goal:** produce evidence that you can do the core work.

Choose one artifact per direction, not a certification collection:

- **Data & tech:** a small analysis, dashboard, or documented automation.
- **Corporate & operations:** a process map, project plan, or policy rewrite.
- **Wellness & care:** a service menu, client intake, or supervised practice
  session.
- **Creative & client:** a niche portfolio piece, pitch, or sample campaign.

Spend no more than 20 hours per artifact. The point is evidence, not perfection.

**Output:** one artifact you can show, plus a one-page explanation of the
problem, the decision, and the result.

## Days 61–75: Test the market

**Goal:** find out whether the market agrees with you.

Use one of these tests:

- Apply to three real roles and track responses.
- Offer a small paid pilot to three people.
- Ask for a shadowing day or a freelance trial.
- Run a 30-minute informational workshop and collect feedback.

Do not rely on friends saying “you would be good at this.” Look for a stranger
who will pay, hire, or refer.

**Output:** three market signals, positive or negative.

## Days 76–90: Decide and package

**Goal:** choose a direction and make the next step obvious.

- Review the evidence from all three tests.
- Score the directions again with your own weights.
- Choose one primary direction and one backup.
- Rewrite your CV or profile around the primary direction.
- Write a 30-second explanation of the transition.
- Ask three people for one specific introduction.
- Set the next 90-day goal.

**Output:** a one-page career decision brief.

## The decision brief

Write no more than one page:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Direction I am choosing | |
| Why this direction, in one sentence | |
| Evidence that supports it | |
| Biggest risk | |

| Income target for year one | |
| Schedule non-negotiable | |
| Next three actions | |
| Decision review date | |

## If you need income now

Run the plan in parallel with a cash-flow job. The cash-flow job is not a
failure. It buys you the time to test. Choose work that protects your energy
and schedule, even if it is not the final direction.

## If you are already burned out

Reduce the plan to three actions:

1. One conversation.
2. One artifact.
3. One application or paid test.

Do not use a career change as the only recovery plan. Rest is not a reward for
finishing the pivot. It is part of the specification.
