# How to use this guide

Most career advice asks you to choose a job title and then hope the life around
it works out. This guide starts from the life you want and works backward.

You will not find a list of “best careers for women” here. You will find a way
to compare work using five signals that actually shape your week: pay, schedule
control, energy cost, entry barrier, and demand. Then you will use those signals
to build a shortlist you can test before you quit anything.

## What this guide is

- A decision tool for women with some work experience who want calmer,
  better-paid work.
- A verified snapshot of 24 roles using BLS OEWS May 2025 pay data and BLS
  Employment Projections 2025–35.
- A comparison system, not a personality quiz.
- A 90-day plan for testing a direction without betting your rent on it.

## What it is not

- A guarantee of a job, salary, visa, remote arrangement, or business income.
- A complete list of every good career. Twenty-four roles are enough to show
  the pattern; they are not the whole labour market.
- A substitute for local licensing, immigration, tax, or legal advice.
- A judgement of the work you do now. The point is to add options, not to
  shame the option you already chose.

## A note on the data

Pay figures are median annual wages for full-time wage and salary workers unless
otherwise stated. They are national figures for the United States. They do not
include bonuses, equity, commission, or the value of benefits. They are not
starting salaries.

The 10th percentile is not a beginner wage. It means 10 percent of workers in
that occupation earn less than that figure. The 90th percentile is not a
promise either. It means 10 percent earn more.

Demand figures are projected annual openings from 2025 to 2035. They include
replacements as people leave the occupation, not only new jobs. A high opening
count can still mean intense competition in your city.

The Soft Life Score is an editorial composite. It is not a government statistic.
The formula is published in Chapter 2 so you can disagree with it and change the
weights for your own life.

> **Data note:** Sources were retrieved in November 2026 from CareerFacts.org,
> which reproduces BLS OEWS May 2025 and BLS Employment Projections 2025–35.
> Direct BLS pages blocked automated requests. Every role page names its SOC
> code and source so you can verify the figure yourself.

## How to read a role profile

Each profile gives you:

- The BLS occupation and SOC code.
- Median pay, the 10th–90th percentile range, projected growth, and annual
  openings.
- Five editorial scores out of 100.
- What makes the work soft, and what makes it hard.
- The first practical move to test the role.

Read the data block first. Then read the two paragraphs. If the numbers and the
reality do not match your life, trust your life.

## Before you begin

Set aside 45 minutes. Have three things ready:

1. Your current annual income, before tax.
2. Your real weekly schedule, including commute, care work, and recovery.
3. A list of the last five workdays that felt sustainable, and the last five
   that did not.

The goal is not to find the perfect career. It is to find three directions
worth testing, then to test them cheaply.
